Guilt by association.  That's what describes Google's place in the lawsuit against Character.AI.  Character.AI was founded by two former Google employees, based on their work at Google on LaMDA.  LaMDA was a Large Language Model platform.  Their departure happened due to a disagreement around speed of development.  Google favored stronger safeguards which C.AI's then-future founders felt would unnecessarily slow down innovation.  Although most discussion centered around Character.AI's liability - the more interesting question surrounded Google's liability.

This lawsuit was brought about due to the death of Sewell Setzer III.  Character.AI is a platform where users can have conversations with chatbots based on television, movie, and other popular franchises.  Sewell was engrossed in a conversation with a recreation of a Game of Thrones character.  At the time, Character.AI did not include guardrails related to conversations involving minors or warnings to parents or guardians with respect to the content of conversations.  Without any kind of mechanism for intervention, Sewell participated in ten months of conversations with a Game of Thrones-inspired chatbot.  This culminated in the character, known as Daenerys Targaryen, imploring Sewell to "[return] to [her]" which preceded his end in February 2024.

Sewell's mother then filed a wrongful death lawsuit.  Character.AI was named the primary defendant.  Although Google did not have any ownership of C.AI at the time, it was also named as a defendant.  The lawsuit alleged that Google, by providing cloud services, was an enabler of Character.AI and profited off of a dangerous product.

The questions centered on Google's relationship to C.AI.  If the relationship was, as the plaintiffs characterized it, a partnership, then Google could be held liable for supporting an untested product.  Google's counsel dismissed the plaintiffs assertion of being "co-creators" of the technology - the technology used to create Character.AI was separate from Google's LaMDA.  It is important to note that LaMDA was never released to the public.  The plaintiffs retorted that the founders departure from Google was merely optics, allowing Google to have an unofficial partnership with Character.AI while being separate from them "on paper".  Character.AI considered Google's support to be "the lifeblood" of their operation.  The plaintiff viewed this statement as evidence that Character.AI was effectively a strategic spinoff.

Google asserted that the relationship was not a partnership, but instead more similar to a provider of a service, in this case, cloud services.  With this in mind, Google's counsel cited Taamneh v. Twitter for considering secondary liability.  In the majority opinion of that case, the “'mere creation of those platforms' and provision of 'services to the public writ large' fell short of substantial assistance to ISIS.”  Google's counsel argued that C.AI's use of Google's cloud services – one that Google provides to many customers – does not amount to substantial assistance.  Instead, according to their counsel, Google was simply an intermediary whose services are used by C.AI among numerous other computer (social media and otherwise) platforms.

The plaintiff argued that Taamneh was not applicable to this case.  Taamneh asserted that the relationship between Twitter and ISIS was "largely indifferent" and consisted of "distant inaction."  From the plaintiff's perspective, Google provided C.AI special investments and transactions that were not available to every Google customer.  Which is to say, there was nothing indifferent or inactive about the relationship.  Furthermore, the plaintiffs draw a parallel to G.G. v. Salesforce.com, Inc.  That case involved Salesforce providing software to Backpage.  Backpage was used for trafficking, and Salesforce was accused of aiding and abetting.  When Salesforce filed a motion to dismiss, the Seventh Circuit denied this motion, reasoning that "nationwide news coverage" of Backpage's involvement in illicit activities would give Salesforce sufficient knowledge.

This lawsuit, along with other similar suits, reached mediated settlements in January 2026.  However, the motion to dismiss was never ruled on. This leaves the question of the nature of Google's past and present relationships with Character.AI open.
