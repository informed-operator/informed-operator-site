About

The operator
behind the signal.

I spent a decade building production software before completing my M.S. in Information Science at the University of Pittsburgh to tackle the security and policy problems I kept encountering from the engineering side. That experience — actually writing the code, building systems, and shipping software that policy frameworks are trying to govern — is the lens through which I write.

I serve as President of the GRC Engineering Club (Pittsburgh Chapter). My work centers on the intersection of software development and programmatic governance: policy-as-code, compliance automation, and technical AI risk management.

Before graduate school, I spent five and a half years as a software consultant at Rivers Agile, delivering full-stack applications for clients in healthcare, robotics, and adtech. Before that, I was an Aircraft Electrical and Environmental Systems Specialist in the U.S. Air Force, maintaining avionics on the F-15. That background — systems troubleshooting under pressure, working from schematics, finding the fault in a complex chain — turns out to be good preparation for security work.

The name Informed Operator comes from that Air Force background. An operator works the systems. An informed one understands the context around them — the law, the policy, the implications. That's the combination I'm building toward, and what this site is about.

What I write about

GRC Engineering & Policy-as-Code — Automating compliance, codifying policy controls (Rego, OPA), and integrating continuous governance directly into CI/CD pipelines and software architecture.

AI Governance & Technical Risk — Practical frameworks and technical controls for AI development. Auditing algorithmic systems, managing training data governance, and implementing hands-on safety guardrails.

Technology Law & Regulatory Policy — Platform regulation, digital evidence standards, and the legal frameworks shaping tech policy — written from the perspective of someone who understands the underlying code.
