About

The operator
behind the signal.

I spent a decade building production software before returning to graduate school to pursue the security and policy problems I kept encountering from the development side. That experience — actually writing the code that contains vulnerabilities, actually shipping the systems that policy frameworks are trying to govern — is the lens through which I write.

I'm currently pursuing an M.S. in Information Science at the University of Pittsburgh, where my work spans two tracks. On the security side: offensive techniques, digital forensics, and incident response — hands-on work in Kali Linux, not just theory. On the policy side: research into AI liability, platform regulation, copyright, autonomous weapons law, and algorithmic discrimination.

Before graduate school, I spent five and a half years as a software consultant at Rivers Agile, delivering full-stack applications for clients in healthcare, robotics, and adtech. Before that, I was an Aircraft Electrical and Environmental Systems Specialist in the U.S. Air Force, maintaining avionics on the F-15. That background — systems troubleshooting under pressure, working from schematics, finding the fault in a complex chain — turns out to be good preparation for security work.

The name Informed Operator comes from that Air Force background. An operator works the systems. An informed one understands the context around them — the law, the policy, the implications. That's the combination I'm building toward, and what this site is about.

What I write about

Cybersecurity — Technical analysis, lessons from lab work, and the practitioner's view of offensive and defensive security. Written for people who want to understand what's actually happening in the systems, not just the headlines.

AI Policy & Governance — The legal and regulatory frameworks trying to keep pace with AI development. Copyright liability, algorithmic discrimination, autonomous systems, training data provenance. I try to bring technical accuracy to these discussions.

Technology Law — Platform regulation, First Amendment limits, digital evidence standards, and the cases shaping how law applies to technology. Analysis grounded in the technical realities most legal commentary glosses over.
